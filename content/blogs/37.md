# The marketing handover your agency needs

A good handover gives a marketing team the context needed to make sensible decisions. Account access is part of it, but access alone does not explain the offer, the audience or the way the business handles demand.

Prepare a small, dependable set of information before work begins. It should help the agency understand the current situation and identify the questions that still need an answer. Keep the material practical enough to maintain as the relationship develops.

## Describe the business as it operates now

Share the priority services, markets, customer types and operational limits. Identify which information is approved and who can confirm specialist details. Old presentations can provide background, but they should not silently become the source for current claims.

Include examples of suitable and unsuitable enquiries. Explain what makes the difference. This gives the agency a clearer picture of customer fit than an audience description built only around broad demographics or company size.

## Organise access and ownership

List the accounts and tools relevant to the agreed scope. Confirm the business owner and the access each person needs. Use the platform's appropriate access process rather than sharing passwords in project notes or messages.

Record where campaign materials, approved images and reporting documents live. The handover should remain understandable when someone is absent or changes role. A clear source of working information reduces repeated requests and confusion.

## Explain the customer journey

Walk through how a prospect discovers the business, makes contact and progresses. Show the forms, booking tools or other steps involved. Describe who responds and what the customer should expect next.

For an illustrative consultation-led business, a submitted form may begin a qualification conversation rather than create a confirmed booking. That distinction affects campaign goals, page wording and reporting. Make it explicit at the beginning.

## Agree the delivery rhythm

Name the people who approve claims, creative and meaningful changes in direction. Set a realistic route for routine questions and a review rhythm that fits the team. Include important business dates or capacity changes that could affect the plan.

Finish with a short list of unresolved questions and their owners. The handover does not need to answer everything, but it should make the unknowns visible.
