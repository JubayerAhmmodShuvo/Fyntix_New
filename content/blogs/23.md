# How an agency and your sales team should work together

Marketing teams can see which campaigns attract a response. Sales teams hear whether that response matches the offer. A useful growth programme connects those views so the next campaign decision reflects the business's experience with actual prospects.

The collaboration does not need to become another complicated meeting structure. Begin with a shared definition of a useful enquiry and a reliable way to record what happens after the first response.

## Agree what good fit looks like

Describe the audience, need, geography and service scope that make an enquiry suitable. Include relevant constraints without turning qualification into an unnecessarily restrictive exercise. The purpose is to give everyone a common reference point.

For example, a business seeking ongoing contracts may still receive legitimate one-off requests. Record that difference clearly so campaign teams can understand which messages attract each type of work.

## Capture a few consistent outcomes

Ask the team handling enquiries to record whether the contact was relevant, whether a conversation took place and what prevented progress where known. Use a small set of meaningful reasons rather than a long form that staff rarely complete.

Keep operational failures visible. A missed call or delayed response is different from an unsuitable prospect. Combining them under a general lost-lead label can send the marketing team towards the wrong fix.

## Turn feedback into specific investigations

If prospects repeatedly misunderstand the service, review the ad and page language. If they ask the same preparation question, improve the relevant content. If one market produces unsuitable requests, inspect the targeting and local information.

Use examples with enough context to support a decision. A single unusual conversation should not automatically redefine the whole campaign, but a recurring pattern deserves attention. Record the change so both teams can assess what happens afterwards.

## Share responsibility for the customer journey

The agency should explain what happens before the enquiry; the business should explain what happens after it. Agree response ownership, availability and the route for urgent questions. A campaign can only support the next step if that step is workable.
