# A marketing plan for businesses with a small internal team

A small team cannot treat every channel as an equal priority. Marketing competes with customer work, operational questions and approvals that only the business can provide. A useful plan respects that capacity instead of assuming an unlimited supply of attention.

Choose a focused customer journey and the offer it supports. Identify the most important gap in discovery, understanding or enquiry. That gives the team a reason to prioritise a small number of connected tasks.

## Build around work the team can support

List the people needed to supply service details, approve claims, answer leads and maintain the website. Estimate their actual availability. A campaign that requires daily creative approvals will struggle if the only approver can review work once a week.

For an illustrative service business, a practical first programme might improve one core service page, answer a handful of recurring customer questions and test a defined advertising offer. The exact combination should follow the diagnosis.

## Batch decisions without losing context

Group related approvals into a predictable review. Provide the background, intended audience and decision needed for each item. This reduces the repeated explanations that make small-team marketing feel more demanding than the work itself.

Keep a short record of approved service language and evidence. Writers and campaign managers can then use dependable information without asking the same factual questions every time they create a new asset.

## Use specialists for defined responsibilities

External support works better when the gap is clear. Ask for technical SEO help when implementation knowledge is missing, campaign management when paid activity needs consistent attention, or content support when useful topics are identified but production stalls.

Assign one internal contact to coordinate that support. The contact does not need to make every decision personally, but should know who can resolve a question and where approved information lives.

## Keep reporting small and actionable

Review a few measures tied to the current objective, together with feedback from the people handling customers. End with the next tasks and their owners. Reserve larger strategy discussions for moments when the evidence or business context warrants them.
