# How to choose a growth partner when your business is scaling

Growth changes the marketing problem. A business that once needed more enquiries may now need a better mix of customers, more consistent demand or a clearer view of acquisition costs. Choosing an agency around the old problem can lead to a programme that produces more work without supporting the next stage.

Before comparing proposals, describe what scaling means for your business. It may involve a new service area, another audience or a larger sales team. Each change creates different questions for search, paid media and content.

## Explain the constraints as well as the ambition

Share the capacity available to handle demand and the characteristics of a valuable customer. Explain where the current process becomes difficult. A useful partner should investigate these limits rather than simply projecting larger traffic or advertising totals.

For example, a growing business may want more recurring contracts while its existing campaigns attract one-off work. The marketing plan should address the offer and qualification process before increasing volume.

## Look for a connected operating plan

Ask how the agency will coordinate search pages, campaign messages, content and enquiry feedback. The proposal should identify the people who make decisions and the information they need. Additional specialists help only when their work has a coherent direction.

Request a sample delivery plan with dependencies. Website changes, business approvals and creative production can all affect a campaign schedule. Making those dependencies visible helps a growing team allocate attention sensibly.

## Discuss how reporting will mature

As the business develops, a simple enquiry total may need support from lead quality, sales progression and acquisition-cost reporting. Ask how the partner would improve that picture without creating an unnecessary administrative burden.

Be clear about what the available data can establish. A business entering a new market may need a learning period before it has a dependable baseline. The agency should distinguish a working assumption from an observed result.

## Test the working relationship

Use an initial investigation or defined programme to assess communication and judgement. Can the agency explain a trade-off clearly? Does it ask useful questions of the sales team? Do reviews lead to understandable actions?
