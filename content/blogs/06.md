# Who should own your marketing decisions?

Marketing decisions often fall between teams. An agency manages the campaigns, a business owner approves spending and sales staff know which enquiries are useful. When those responsibilities are unclear, everyone can complete their own tasks while the wider programme loses direction.

The solution is a simple decision structure. Define who sets the commercial goal, who can change the work and who provides the information needed for review. Keep that structure small enough that people will use it during a busy week.

## Give the business goal an owner

A senior person in the business should confirm the offer, customer priorities and operational constraints. An agency can challenge and advise, but it needs a dependable source for decisions about service capacity, geographic coverage and the type of customer the business wants to attract.

Write these choices down. If a campaign targets a service the team cannot currently deliver, the issue should be visible before the advertisement goes live, rather than discovered during the reporting meeting.

## Separate delivery from approval

The people producing articles, improving search pages and managing ads need enough authority to complete agreed work. Define which changes can happen within the plan and which need approval. Keep the approval process proportionate to the decision.

For example, correcting an inaccurate business address is different from launching an untested offer. A useful agreement makes routine work straightforward while preserving business oversight of meaningful commitments.

## Give sales feedback a route back

Ask the sales or service team to record a small set of consistent outcomes. Was the enquiry relevant? Could the business help? Did the prospect progress to a conversation or purchase? Agree who reviews those observations and how campaign teams will receive them.

The feedback does not need to become a large reporting exercise. A reliable, concise record is more useful than a detailed form that staff complete only occasionally. Its purpose is to improve the next decision.

## End reviews with named actions

At each review, identify what will change, why and who owns it. Note the information needed before the next discussion. A decision log creates continuity when a team member is absent or a campaign develops over several months.
