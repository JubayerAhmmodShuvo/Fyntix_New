# Turn an SEO audit into improvements that ship

An SEO audit can identify a large number of possible improvements. The challenge is turning that information into useful changes on the live website. Without prioritisation and implementation ownership, the audit becomes a growing list rather than a plan.

Start with the pages and customer journeys that matter most to the business. A technical issue affecting a core service page may deserve earlier attention than a cosmetic inconsistency across low-priority articles. The explanation should connect the finding with its practical consequence.

## Separate diagnosis from the proposed fix

For each important item, record what was observed, which pages are affected and why the issue matters. Then specify a proposed change. This makes the recommendation easier for a developer, writer or business owner to assess.

For example, an audit might identify several pages competing to explain the same service. The next step is to review their intended audiences and usefulness before deciding whether to improve, consolidate or retain them. A tool warning alone does not make that decision.

## Prioritise by effect and dependency

Group tasks around access and indexation, page relevance, internal structure and the visitor's next step. Identify dependencies between them. If a priority page cannot be discovered as intended, publishing more supporting articles may be a lower immediate priority.

Add a realistic estimate of effort and the person needed to approve or implement the change. A recommendation that requires an unavailable team should be discussed openly rather than left indefinitely at the top of the list.

## Define how completion will be checked

An implemented ticket is not the same as a verified improvement. Confirm that the intended change appears on the live page and that related functionality still works. Keep a record of the pages changed and the date of release.

Review the relevant search and website measures over a suitable period. Movement can have several causes, so distinguish the completed work from any claim about its effect. Avoid declaring a ranking outcome from a single short snapshot.

## Maintain a small active work queue

Keep the next few tasks clear, with owners and review points. Revisit the backlog as business priorities change or new evidence appears. A useful audit remains connected to delivery rather than being treated as a fixed document.
