# Build a marketing report that supports decisions

A marketing report should help the business understand what happened and choose what to do next. Large collections of charts can make that task harder when the reader cannot tell which measures matter or why a comparison was chosen.

Start with the decisions the review needs to support. The report can then organise the relevant evidence around those questions, with enough detail to inspect the result without requiring everyone to navigate a platform export.

## Give each measure a clear meaning

Define the actions and reporting scope. A search impression, website visit, submitted enquiry and customer describe different stages. Keep them separate and explain how the team uses each measure.

For an illustrative consultation business, selecting a booking time is not necessarily the same event as receiving a confirmed appointment. A useful report preserves that distinction instead of combining the events into a larger but less understandable total.

## Make comparisons readable

Show the reporting window and the basis of the comparison. Explain changes in budget, offer, tracking or service coverage that materially affect interpretation. Where periods differ, state the difference rather than relying on a headline percentage alone.

Charts should help readers see the relationship between the figures. Use a scale appropriate to the comparison and retain important qualifiers such as estimates or rounded totals. A clear table can be more useful than a decorative visual that obscures the measure.

## Separate observation from interpretation

Begin with what the information shows. Then explain the likely implications and what remains uncertain. A rise following a website change does not, by itself, establish that the change caused the whole result.

Use customer and sales feedback to add context. If the team suspects a lead-quality issue, describe the evidence and the investigation needed. This makes uncertainty actionable rather than leaving it as a vague caveat.

## End with a manageable work plan

List the next decisions, their owners and the information needed for review. Connect the list with the findings in the report. Keep a record so the next meeting can assess what happened after the previous actions.

Fyntix reports on search visibility, paid media and content in the context of the business's growth goals. The report should make that connection easy to read: a defined question, understandable evidence and a practical next step. Its value comes from the decisions it supports, rather than the number of pages it contains.
