# Growth agency or in-house team: choosing the right mix

The agency-versus-in-house discussion is often framed as a choice between two complete solutions. In practice, the more useful question is how to divide responsibility. Your business holds customer knowledge and commercial priorities; specialists can contribute research, channel expertise and delivery capacity.

Start by mapping the work you need, rather than choosing a team structure first. Search visibility, paid media, content production and reporting each require decisions, implementation and feedback. Identify which of those responsibilities already have a capable owner.

## Keep essential business knowledge close

Someone inside the business should own service information, audience priorities and the definition of a useful enquiry. Those choices cannot be delegated effectively without an ongoing relationship. An external team needs access to the people who understand how customers evaluate and buy.

For example, an agency may identify strong interest in a particular service. The business still needs to confirm whether it has capacity, whether the work is commercially suitable and what a prospect should be told.

## Match specialists to the actual gap

If the internal team can produce good content but lacks a search plan, targeted SEO support may be more useful than outsourcing the whole editorial process. If campaign demand is growing faster than the team can manage, paid media support may address a capacity problem.

Be specific about the deliverable. A defined audit, campaign-management scope or content programme is easier to assess than a broad promise to become an extension of the team.

## Design the handover between people

Agree how briefs are approved, how factual questions reach the right person and how sales feedback returns to campaign teams. Use a shared work plan with clear owners. The benefit of specialist help can disappear when every task waits for an undefined approval.

Document account access and the location of working materials from the beginning. A healthy arrangement should remain understandable when a staff member changes role or a project moves into a different phase.

## Review the arrangement as the business develops

An early-stage business may need a small, connected group of specialists. A larger team may need support in a specific discipline or during a concentrated delivery period. Revisit the structure when the workload, customer journey or internal capability changes.
