# Hire a specialist or fix the marketing plan first?

When marketing stalls, hiring another specialist can feel like the obvious next step. Sometimes the business does need more capability. Sometimes the missing piece is a clear offer, an agreed priority or a decision nobody currently owns.

Before writing a role description or requesting proposals, describe the work that is not happening. Be specific about the gap and what prevents it from moving. That makes it easier to distinguish a capacity problem from a planning problem.

## Identify the blocked work

List a few concrete examples. Perhaps search recommendations are waiting for implementation, useful content briefs never reach publication, or paid campaigns run without feedback about enquiry quality. Ask which part of the process is missing: knowledge, time, approval or access.

For an illustrative example, a business may have a capable writer but no agreed audience or service priority. Hiring another writer would increase production capacity without resolving the editorial decision that blocks useful work.

## Check whether the offer is settled

A specialist needs a dependable commercial context. Confirm what the business wants to sell, who should buy it and how a prospect progresses. If those answers keep changing, give the team a process for resolving them rather than expecting the new hire to infer them.

This does not mean every strategic question must be settled permanently. It means the work needs an explicit set of current assumptions and a person who can approve changes as evidence develops.

## Match support to the responsibility

A defined technical SEO investigation, campaign-management scope or content programme may address the gap more directly than a broad job title. Compare the ongoing workload, the range of skills needed and the internal capacity to manage the relationship.

Consider the dependencies the specialist will still need. More expertise will not remove the need for accurate service information, usable account access or someone to respond to enquiries. Include those responsibilities in the plan.

## Review the arrangement through delivery

Agree what should become easier after the new support begins. Useful indicators include a clearer work queue, changes reaching the live site and reporting that informs decisions. Avoid judging the arrangement only by the amount of output produced.
