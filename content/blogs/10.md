# Make your offer the starting point for paid campaigns

An advertisement cannot resolve an unclear offer. It can attract attention, but the customer still needs to understand what is being offered, why it fits their need and what happens after they respond. Those decisions should come before campaign settings and creative production.

Write the offer as something a customer can evaluate. Replace internal service labels with a practical explanation. Include the information that affects fit, particularly where an enquiry consumes staff time or where the business serves a limited market.

## Define the useful response

Decide what the campaign should help a suitable customer do. The action might be requesting a quote, booking a consultation or purchasing a product. Explain how that action connects to the business's sales process and who will respond.

A broad instruction to generate leads is incomplete. For an illustrative home-service campaign, a relevant quote request may need a service location and a brief description of the job. Those details help the team assess suitability without making the first interaction unnecessarily complicated.

## Match the promise to the destination

The landing page should carry through the message that earned the click. If the ad promotes a specific service, make that service easy to find. Explain the scope and show evidence that helps a visitor assess it. Use a clear call to action with an understandable consequence.

Avoid introducing a different offer on the page simply because it is more convenient for the business to promote. A change in direction can leave the customer unsure whether they have arrived in the right place.

## Identify the question each creative tests

A creative test is more useful when it explores a reason someone might respond. Compare meaningful ideas: convenience, specialist knowledge, a clearer process or a specific customer concern. Cosmetic variations can be helpful later, but they should not replace the discussion about the offer.

Keep the test manageable. Record what differs between versions and what response you will review. The right amount of testing depends on the audience, budget and information available.

## Use feedback to refine the offer

Read enquiry quality alongside campaign activity. If the message attracts unsuitable customers, revisit the promise and qualification details. If suitable people arrive but do not continue, review the information and friction after the click.
