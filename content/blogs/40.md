# Specialist growth agency or large consultancy?

The useful comparison between a specialist agency and a large consultancy begins with the work. A business may need hands-on campaign delivery, a focused search investigation or a wider organisational programme. Those needs call for different arrangements.

Describe the decisions and implementation required before comparing providers. The right partner should be able to explain its role, the people involved and the dependencies it expects your business to manage.

## Define the boundary of the engagement

Identify whether the problem sits mainly within marketing delivery or across a broader set of business responsibilities. A search-and-campaign programme may need close contact with sales and website teams while remaining a focused scope. Another project may require wider coordination.

For an illustrative example, improving local service enquiries is different from redesigning a company's entire customer operation. A provider should explain how its proposed scope fits the actual problem rather than expanding the brief by default.

## Compare the working arrangement

Ask who will perform the work, who makes decisions and how information reaches the delivery team. Consider the availability of the people responsible for implementation. A strong presentation does not describe the day-to-day relationship on its own.

Discuss how changes will move from recommendation to release. Clarify where the provider implements directly, where it advises and where another team remains responsible. This distinction matters regardless of the organisation's size.

## Understand the cost of coordination

Every engagement requires attention from the client. Review the meetings, approvals and specialist input needed to keep the work moving. Choose a process that fits the internal team's capacity and the importance of the decisions involved.

Ask for the reasoning behind the reporting structure. A compact campaign review and a wider programme steering meeting serve different purposes. The arrangement should make decisions easier rather than create a reporting layer without a clear job.

## Evaluate through a concrete first phase

Where appropriate, define an initial investigation or delivery period with reviewable outputs. Assess the quality of judgement, communication and implementation. That gives the business more evidence than a broad comparison based on company size alone.
