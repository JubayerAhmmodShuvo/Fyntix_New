# Find the bottleneck before changing your marketing channel

A disappointing sales period can lead quickly to a discussion about replacing a channel. Sometimes that is the right decision. First, identify where the customer journey is weakening and what the available information can establish.

Break the journey into a few understandable stages: relevant discovery, a useful visit, a meaningful response and follow-up. Each stage raises different questions. A channel change may leave the original problem untouched if the gap sits elsewhere.

## Check whether the right people can find you

Review the audiences, search questions and markets reaching the business. Consider whether important services have a useful presence. If discovery is weak, the priority may involve search pages, campaign reach or content that addresses a relevant need.

For an illustrative regional provider, growing traffic from an unsuitable area does not resolve a shortage of local enquiries. The first diagnosis should distinguish reach from customer fit.

## Review what visitors understand

Follow the main routes into the website. Check whether the offer, evidence and next step match the visitor's expectation. A campaign can bring appropriate people to a page that does not answer their practical questions.

Use enquiry and sales conversations to find missing information. If prospects repeatedly misunderstand the scope, review the message across the whole journey. Changing the source of traffic may reproduce the same misunderstanding.

## Inspect the response and handover

Complete the enquiry process and observe what happens afterwards. Check form function, confirmation and the route to the person responsible. Review whether enquiries are reached and whether the follow-up makes the next step clear.

Separate unsuitable demand from a missed opportunity to respond. Those problems require different actions, and combining them under a general conversion issue can hide an operational bottleneck.

## Choose a focused next investigation

Once the likely gap is identified, define the information needed to test it. Make a reviewable change and record the context. The aim is to reduce uncertainty enough to choose the next step, rather than rebuild every channel at once.

Fyntix connects search visibility, paid media and content with the wider enquiry journey. A useful growth plan begins by locating the problem, then choosing work that addresses it. That makes channel decisions more understandable and gives the business a clearer explanation of what it is trying to improve.
