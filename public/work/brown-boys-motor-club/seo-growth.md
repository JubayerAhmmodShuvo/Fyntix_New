# Brown Boys Motor Club: SEO Growth

Automotive · Local & technical SEO · Six-month programme

A six-month search programme for an Oshawa dealership, connecting technical improvements, local relevance and useful buying information.

## Results overview

Google Search Console compares the previous six months with the following six months. The report does not give calendar start and end dates.

| Measure | Previous six months | Following six months | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Organic clicks | 375 | 2,290 | +510% reported |
| Search impressions | 4,360 | 142,000 | +3,157% reported |
| Average position | 13.5 | 7.5 | 6 positions better |
| Average CTR | 8.6% | 1.6% | −7 percentage points |

Displayed totals imply 1,915 additional clicks and 137,640 additional impressions: approximately 6.1 times as many clicks and 32.6 times as many impressions. Uplifts are rounded. No paid advertising was used for the programme.

## Help local car buyers find their next option.

Brown Boys Motor Club buys, sells, trades and finances pre-owned vehicles in Oshawa, Ontario. Its search programme focused on local discovery across the surrounding Durham Region.

A vehicle search can begin in several places: a preferred model, a nearby dealer or a question about financing. Each starting point needs a useful answer and a clear next step. A dealership’s website can connect those needs to the relevant inventory and service information.

The commercial objective is a better route from a search to an informed enquiry. This case measures the discovery stage of that journey: how frequently the website appeared, how often people clicked and where its results appeared on average.

## A local business. A narrow search footprint.

The starting audit identified slow mobile pages, weak page structure, limited location targeting and an incomplete Google Business Profile. Inventory content and internal links also needed attention.

For a dealership, those issues touch the same customer journey. Someone arriving on a phone needs to understand the vehicle, find relevant information and continue exploring without unnecessary friction. A city page should make the business’s local offer clear, while an inventory page should explain the individual vehicle.

The starting figures provided a practical benchmark. With 375 clicks over the earlier six-month window, the site had room to reach a wider audience. The average position of 13.5 also pointed to an opportunity to improve visibility across the overall query mix.

## Make every part of discovery work together.

### Strengthen the foundation

Mobile image optimisation, crawl fixes, sitemap submission, canonical checks and structured data addressed the technical base.

This stage gives the rest of the programme a usable foundation. Clear page relationships help people explore the site, while consistent technical signals make the intended pages easier to discover.

### Connect pages with intent

Location-aware titles, headings, inventory descriptions, internal links and FAQs sharpened page relevance.

A page has a clearer purpose when its topic matches the question that brought someone there. Useful vehicle details and a logical route to related inventory can support the next step in that decision.

### Build local consistency

Business Profile improvements, customer-review follow-up and consistent Canadian directory listings supported local presence.

People compare businesses across several surfaces. Consistent business information makes that comparison simpler and reduces uncertainty about which dealership they are looking at.

### Answer buying questions

Local landing pages and Ontario buying guides extended the site’s coverage of relevant searches.

Educational information can help a visitor evaluate an option before making contact. The useful connection is between the question, a relevant answer and the part of the business that can help.

## More clicks. A lower rate. Both can be true.

Click-through rate is a ratio: clicks divided by impressions. A much larger denominator can reduce that ratio even when the numerator grows. Here, impressions expanded faster than clicks, so the lower aggregate CTR is consistent with a much broader search footprint.

The figures make that relationship visible. The earlier period combined 375 clicks with 4,360 impressions, approximately 8.6%. The later period combined 2,290 clicks with 142,000 impressions, approximately 1.6%. The rounded rates describe two very different levels of exposure.

That does not make every new impression equally valuable. A useful follow-up is to separate branded and non-branded queries, review important landing pages and compare mobile performance. That would show where additional visibility is translating into relevant visits and where titles, content or rankings still need work.

The strongest supported conclusion is greater discovery through organic search. Enquiries, test drives and vehicle sales would require their own tracking before those outcomes could be attached to this campaign.

## Turn stronger discovery into a clearer business picture.

The next stage is a measurement plan, rather than an additional set of claimed results.

### Prioritise useful visits

Review which search terms and landing pages are attracting prospective buyers. Rising exposure is most useful when the page answers the intent behind the visit.

### Connect discovery to enquiries

Measure calls, finance-form completions and test-drive requests as distinct actions. Keep duplicate events and completed sales separate so the report reflects the customer journey.

### Keep inventory journeys current

Review the experience when a vehicle sells or a listing changes. Help visitors continue to relevant available options and keep the site’s navigation useful.

## The numbers, with their context.

These are aggregate organic search comparisons. The report gives the duration of each window without calendar start and end dates. The charts therefore compare two six-month totals rather than presenting an invented monthly trend.

The larger totals in the report are rounded. Derived differences and multiples here use those displayed figures. Clicks are search-result interactions, impressions are appearances and position is an average; they describe different parts of discovery.
