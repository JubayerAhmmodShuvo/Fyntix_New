# Landing pages that help ad visitors take the next step

Google Ads · Fyntix Editorial · Updated 24 Nov 2026

Connect the advertisement, the page and the enquiry route so a suitable visitor can understand the offer and act with confidence.

A landing page should continue the conversation started by an advertisement. A visitor arrives with an expectation about the offer. The page needs to confirm that expectation, provide the information needed to assess it and make the next step understandable.

Before changing the design, identify the customer decision the page supports. A quote request, consultation and direct purchase need different information. Choose the structure around that decision rather than copying a layout because it performed well for another business.

## Make the first screen useful

Explain the offer in clear language and show who it helps. Include relevant qualification details, such as service area or intended customer type. A visitor should be able to recognise that they have reached the right place without interpreting an abstract headline.

The main call to action should describe a practical step. If it opens a form, explain what the team needs and what happens afterwards. The wording can reduce uncertainty before the visitor begins.

## Answer the questions that delay action

Use sales feedback to identify the information prospects commonly request. Scope, process, availability and evidence may deserve a place before the form. Prioritise the questions that help someone judge suitability rather than adding every detail the business could publish.

For an illustrative consultation page, a short explanation of the discussion and the preparation required may be more helpful than a broad claim about transformation. The page should make the proposed interaction concrete.

## Make evidence easy to assess

Use relevant examples, service detail and approved customer evidence. Keep the context of a result visible, especially its period and the measure being reported. A headline number becomes more useful when the reader understands what it describes.

Give the page enough structure for scanning while allowing an interested visitor to read further. Clear headings and concise sections can support both behaviours without forcing the whole page into short slogans.

## Test the action and review the response

Complete the journey on a phone and another relevant device. Check that the form works, required fields make sense and confirmation is clear. Review the enquiries with the team handling them, because a higher submission rate alone does not establish better customer fit.
