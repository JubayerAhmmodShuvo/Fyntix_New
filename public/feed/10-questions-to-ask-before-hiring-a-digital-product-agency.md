# 10 questions to ask a growth agency

Growth strategy · Fyntix Editorial · Updated 24 Nov 2026

Ten questions that reveal how an agency connects search, paid media and content with the customers your business needs.

A useful agency conversation starts with your business, rather than a list of channels. Before discussing SEO, Google Ads or content production, establish what you sell, who should buy it and what currently prevents the right people from becoming customers.

The questions below help turn a sales presentation into a working discussion. Ask for concrete examples of the proposed process, the decisions you will share and the evidence you will review. A clear answer should make the relationship easier to assess before you commit.

## Start with the commercial problem

First, ask: **Which customer problem would you investigate first?** A considered answer should connect the offer, audience and buying journey. Second: **What would success mean for our business?** Agree whether the priority is qualified enquiries, booked consultations, purchases or another defined action. Third: **What do you need from our sales team?** An agency should want to understand which enquiries progress and which do not.

For example, a local service business may receive plenty of calls from outside its service area. More calls alone would miss the problem. The initial work might instead examine geographic targeting, service-page wording and the way callers are qualified.

## Understand how the work connects

Fourth: **Why these channels, in this order?** The proposal should explain the relationship between existing demand and the testing budget. Fifth: **Who improves the experience after the click?** Ad copy, page information and the enquiry route need an owner. Sixth: **How will content support discovery and decisions?** Ask for a sample brief with a reader, a question and a relevant next step.

These answers reveal whether the agency can connect Search & AI Visibility with Paid Media & Content. A channel can be well managed while the wider customer journey still feels disconnected.

## Make measurement and ownership clear

Seventh: **Which actions will the report count?** Separate a form submission from a qualified opportunity. Eighth: **Who controls the accounts and data?** Clarify access, documentation and the handover process. Ninth: **How will we decide what changes next?** Look for a regular review with named actions. Tenth: **What would make you revise the plan?** Useful partners can explain uncertainty and the evidence that would change their recommendation.

## Leave with something reviewable

Ask the shortlisted agency to outline its first investigation, the information it needs and a sensible order of work. You should be able to understand why those tasks matter without needing to interpret a page of acronyms.
