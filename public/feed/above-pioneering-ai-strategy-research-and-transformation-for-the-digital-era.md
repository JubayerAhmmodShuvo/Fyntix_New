# How to turn a growth audit into a 90-day plan

Growth strategy · Fyntix Editorial · Updated 24 Nov 2026

Turn an audit into a focused sequence of search, advertising and content improvements that a team can actually deliver.

An audit is useful when it changes the order of work. A long inventory of technical issues, campaign observations and content gaps can leave a team with the same uncertainty it had before the review. The next step is to decide which problems deserve attention first.

A 90-day plan provides a manageable working horizon. It is a way to organise delivery and review, rather than a guarantee that every business will reach the same result within three months. The right priorities depend on the starting position and the customer's buying cycle.

## Translate findings into business questions

Rewrite each important finding as a question. A weak service page becomes: can a suitable prospect understand what is included and how to enquire? Irrelevant search traffic becomes: which customer needs should this page serve? Incomplete conversion reporting becomes: can the team distinguish an enquiry from a qualified opportunity?

This translation helps people outside marketing assess the work. It also prevents a technical task from remaining on the plan simply because it appeared in an audit export.

## Choose dependencies before quick wins

Identify what must be in place before the next task becomes useful. Campaign testing needs a suitable destination and a meaningful response to measure. A content brief needs an agreed reader and offer. Reporting needs consistent event definitions and access to the relevant information.

For example, a business with a broken enquiry form should address that route before expanding paid traffic to it. The dependency is clear even if other tasks look more exciting in a presentation.

## Organise a realistic sequence

Use the early period to establish access, measurement and priority page improvements. Follow with a limited set of search, campaign or content tests. Reserve later reviews for evaluating what has been learned and deciding which improvements deserve further investment.

Give each task an owner, a review point and an explanation of what completion means. Include the client's responsibilities, such as confirming service information or providing sales feedback. A plan built entirely around agency tasks can stall when those dependencies remain invisible.

## Make the final review useful

At the end of the period, compare the work completed with the original questions. Explain which uncertainties were reduced and which remain. Keep changes in traffic, enquiry quality and customer behaviour separate so the team can read the result accurately.

At Fyntix, a growth audit should lead to a practical roadmap across Search & AI Visibility and Paid Media & Content. The deliverable is a sequence of decisions and improvements that the business understands, rather than a document that sits beside the work.
