# A practical SEO checklist before you publish

SEO · Fyntix Editorial · Updated 24 Nov 2026

Review the purpose, structure, links and next step of an important page before adding it to your website.

A page can be technically published while still being difficult to understand or disconnected from the rest of the website. A short review before release helps a team catch those gaps while they are straightforward to fix.

Use this checklist for an important service page or article. Adapt it to the page's purpose, rather than treating every item as a reason to delay publication. The aim is a useful page with a clear place in the site and a sensible way to assess it afterwards.

## Confirm the reader and the question

Write down who the page helps and the question it answers. Check whether the opening gives that person enough context to continue. If the page tries to address several unrelated decisions, consider whether the material needs a clearer focus.

Compare the proposed page with existing content. Another page may already serve the same purpose and deserve an update. Make the publishing decision around usefulness, rather than the pressure to fill an editorial calendar.

## Review the visible information

Check the title, main heading and supporting sections for clarity. Confirm the service details, claims, examples and contact information with the relevant people. Use descriptive wording for images and links where it helps the reader understand their purpose.

A service page should explain what a customer can expect. An educational article should answer its question sufficiently before asking the reader to make contact. Both benefit from an obvious structure and language that the intended audience understands.

## Check access and the page's place in the site

Ask the appropriate specialist to confirm that the page is accessible as intended and that publishing settings match its purpose. Review internal links from relevant pages so the new content has a useful route into the website.

Open the page on a phone and follow its main links. Check for unreadable text, broken images, obstructed controls and forms that are difficult to use. These are practical publishing checks even before considering search performance.

## Define the next step and review point

Give the reader a relevant continuation: a related service, another explanation or an enquiry route. Make the action understandable. Then decide what the team will review after publication, such as relevant search queries, visits to the page or suitable enquiries.

Keep a brief release note with the page owner and review date. Fyntix uses content structure, technical SEO and internal linking as connected parts of search work. A publishing checklist helps those responsibilities meet before a page goes live.
